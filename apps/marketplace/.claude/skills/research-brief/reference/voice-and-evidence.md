# Voice and evidence — Operator OS articles

## Who's talking

Nathan Baker, founder and systems architect of AutoNateAI: a software engineer and business analyst (University of Michigan CS; Microsoft, Citi, Veterans United, Atomic Object) who built his own conversational operating system — a weekly attention graph, a prospect/client/partner relationship graph, research memory, a live cockpit — and now installs it for operators. First person ("I"), occasionally "we" for AutoNateAI. Don't use the old Michigan-to-Bootheel family bio; it's retired from the site.

## Who's listening

The owner who *is* the business: the HVAC/electrical/plumbing/roofing contractor, the broker or property manager, the consultant, the nonprofit director, the local B2B operator. Smart, busy, operationally excellent, not an engineer. They've been sold software before and it made their week longer.

## Write to wake them up

The article's job is recognition, then relief. Tactics that work:

- **Open inside their day**, not inside a statistic. A specific scene they have lived: the voicemail from Tuesday they still haven't returned, the estimate that sat in the truck for four days, the Sunday night spent reconciling three apps. Then land the number.
- **Name the thing they've been blaming on themselves.** Most owners think the problem is discipline or hustle. Show them it's architecture — they are the integration layer between tools that don't talk. That reframe is the whole emotional payload.
- **Put a dollar figure or an hour figure on the hidden tax**, with the math shown. "You're not losing leads, you're losing $4,200 a month in estimates nobody followed up on — here's the arithmetic" beats any adjective.
- **Second person is fine in the diagnosis** ("you," "your truck," "your inbox"); keep it respectful — never mock the reader, mock the situation.
- **Short sentences at the turns.** Let a one-line paragraph hit.
- **Confident, plainspoken, a little funny.** Dry humor is welcome; corporate hedging is not. "Your CRM is a very expensive place to feel guilty" is the register. Professional throughout — a bank or a franchise owner should be able to forward this without embarrassment.
- **No hype words**: no "revolutionize," "game-changer," "unlock," "supercharge," "leverage AI." No em-dash-heavy AI cadence; vary the rhythm. No "In today's fast-paced world."
- **The product appears late and specifically.** The Operator OS shows up in the "system intervention" section as a concrete mechanism (what the conversation writes, what the graph stores, what the cockpit shows, what the agent does), not as a pitch. The CTA section is the only place that asks for anything.

## Evidence bar

- Every number has a URL you actually loaded, and a year. Vendor surveys are labeled as the vendor's own ("ServiceTitan's 2025 survey of its own customers found…").
- Don't launder old stats. If the best study is from 2011, say "a 2011 study" and explain why the mechanism still holds.
- Don't stretch a stat across populations. A study of B2B web leads is not a study of HVAC phone calls — say what it measured and why it's (or isn't) a fair proxy.
- When no real number exists, **model it**: a before/after table with every assumption in plain view ("assume 60 inbound leads a month, a 35% close rate on followed-up estimates, $6,500 average install…"). Label models as models. Let the reader swap in their own numbers — say "plug in yours."
- Never invent a customer, a quote, a case study, or a result. AutoNateAI's own system is fair to describe (it exists); client results are not, until there are real, approved ones.
- Never name real prospects or clients (no Kent, Tiras Realty, Urban Housing Solutions, "Auntie E"). Use archetypes or clearly fictional names.

## What never appears

- Private Airtable bases, record IDs, internal task names, or the internal attention-graph data.
- Prices other than what's already public on /pricing.
- Claims that the Operator OS guarantees revenue outcomes.

## Moral of the Story

Every article ends (before the CTA) with a `## Moral of the Story` of concrete, usable moves the reader can make **this week, with or without us** — the free value is what earns the call. One to five items, each specific enough to act on ("Tonight, count every lead from the last 30 days that never got a second touch. That number is your follow-up ceiling."). This is the section people screenshot.
